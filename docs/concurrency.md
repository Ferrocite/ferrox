# Concurrency model

One decision, stated with its checker. This is the design answer to the thread/kernel-CPU tax the method pages name and batching does not remove. The raw TCP data path has it and is measured; the carriered legs run the fixed relay lane pool, and their accept handshakes run a separate setup pool described below.

## The tax, honestly

On its carriered legs Ferrox is a **pooled blocking relay**: `lane_pool` (`crates/ferrox-app/src/proxy.rs`) runs each direction a step at a time; a step is one read and the write it feeds, a lane with bytes keeps its worker for a streak of eight steps and then rejoins the queue, and a read that finds nothing parks the lane off the workers. A socket lane parks on the descriptor its timed-out read named, in a reaper that polls every parked descriptor: the peer's byte wakes the lane at once, and silence costs one step per `RELAY_RESPONSE_IDLE` (one second, the budget that notices a sibling which ended while this direction stayed quiet). A lane whose source is not a socket parks in `parked`, where one worker pays the wait for the whole batch and the wait doubles per empty round up to 16 ms. A carrier whose window is full comes back on the next turn instead of a quantum later. Idle workers cost nothing, but a leg blocked in `read` holds its worker for the wait, so the number of lanes is the number of reads that can be outstanding at once. That ceiling is eight, one per flow of the widest standard cell: every download read must sit in its read at once or the peer's window stops refilling, and seven left the eighth flow queued at 141 MiB/s where eight measures 158 against the reference server's 170 in run `40685674043`. A worker is started only when a queued lane finds none idle, so a one-flow cell holds two: four lanes measured 365/402/430 MiB/s on `trojan-tls-ws`/`vless-grpc`/`vless-httpupgrade` in run `c56602be`, where the thread-per-direction tree's 18/11/11 threads measured 371/478/532 on run `40418120113` — the lanes are fewer than the threads and carry the cell.

`xray-core` does not pay this: a goroutine blocked on a read is descheduled by the runtime netpoller, and a per-connection goroutine is ~2 KiB of heap, not a kernel thread.

## Why buffered batching is the wrong tool for *this* tax

Batching is real and it landed: `CHUNKS_PER_BATCH` folds four chunks into one `readv`/`writev` on Shadowsocks, and gRPC/xHTTP coalesce a frame's parts into one socket call per 256 KiB read. Every one of those cuts **syscalls per byte** — the throughput axis. None touches **threads per concurrency**: a batched read that returns quickly still blocks its thread when there is nothing to read, and coalescing writes never un-parks an idle leg. The two taxes are orthogonal; more micro-batching cannot close this one.

## The chosen design: a readiness seam, not an async rewrite

The seam sits *under* the `Read`/`Write` contract, so nothing above it changes. On Linux the raw legs reuse the `splice` pump: a direction splices source to pipe and pipe to destination, one `poll` wake on either descriptor driving both legs. Where there is no `splice`, `poll_copy` keeps the same shape with a 64 KiB buffered copy and `poll`/`WSAPoll`; a wake pipe (Unix) or loopback TCP pair (Windows) releases a worker when a flow is queued. Both pools are fixed at `min(available_parallelism, 8)` workers and both hand a flow back only when both directions have ended.

`relay_handoff` clones the four descriptors, arms them non-blocking, queues the flow and returns; the accept loop's thread is finished with the flow before the first byte moves. The guards and completion channels live in the `Flow`, so the sockets' blocking mode is restored and the joiner woken by the workers, not by a caller parked on a channel. `Pump` uses a one-shot `RelayPool::run_once` for the same reason. This is the M:N trick the goroutine netpoller runs — **N legs ride ~`cores` worker threads** — releasing the threads parked in `read`, never the ones actively copying.

A masked leg, or a host where the arm fails, still falls back to one spawned blocking relay thread; that is the safe path, not the measured one.

## Rejected, and why

- **Adopt tokio / rewrite to `async`.** A second scheduler whose task wakes are the kernel CPU being removed, a whole-tree rewrite risking the byte-identity differential, and a violation of "no second way of doing something". The poller takes the same M:N win behind `Read`/`Write` with no signature churn.
- **Thread-per-leg with caps or smaller stacks.** A ceiling turns the tax into dropped connections; smaller stacks do not remove the context switch.
- **More buffered batching.** The wrong axis, shown above.
- **The TLS-raw fixed-worker poll relay, measured and rejected.** One 64 KiB non-blocking `poll` direction under the same seam cut threads 35 → 11 on `vless-tls-raw-down-8` (ubuntu-24.04-arm, five repeats) but lost to main on all three axes: 1963–2000 vs 2060–2104 MiB/s, 1611–1639 vs 1598–1648 CPU ms/GiB, 12.9 vs 11.8 MiB RSS. A 16 KiB window cut RSS further and fell harder; 128 KiB bought nothing. Thread count alone is not worth a throughput/CPU/RSS regression; the next attempt needs >16 flows (issue #387) or a shape that keeps main's byte path.

## The checker

A duration suggests; a thread count proves. `threads_peak` per engine per cell is recorded beside the paired throughput and CPU intervals in the same run. Measured cell `vless-raw-down-8` at five repeats; one-flow control `vless-raw-down-1`:

| cell | ferrox before | ferrox now | xray-core | zeronet |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ubuntu x86_64, 8 connections | 31 | 7 | 17 | 5 |
| ubuntu aarch64, 8 connections | — | 7 | 17 | 5 |
| windows x86_64, 8 connections | 39 | 11 | 11 | 9 |
| macOS aarch64, 8 connections | — | 6 | 19 | 4 |

At one connection the peak is **7** (both Ubuntu runners) and **11** (Windows) — the same as at eight; the number does not move with the flow count. Throughput gate passed in every cell (paired ferrox/xray-core midpoints 2.13×, 2.63×, 1.20×, and 1.28× at eight ties and 1.08× five-way median on macOS); CPU per GiB and peak RSS are lower than xray-core in the same cells.

The **idle-leg** half is still open for the carriered paths below the VMess pump: the cell above carries active traffic, proving the fixed peak for raw TCP only. The pages' per-peer `pinned-threads` rows are the checker there, and `idle-legs-pinned-threads-per-connection` stays `UNBLESSED` until a carrier cell reads it. VMess's `a_live_vmess_session_costs_no_thread` and Shadowsocks's `a_live_session_costs_no_thread` (one in each of `shadowsocks` and `shadowsocks2022`) are the exceptions: each holds eight sessions with no traffic at all, which is the idle-leg shape for those pumps.

## Status

`landed and measured` for the raw TCP data path: vless/vmess/trojan raw, the SOCKS client and freedom direct paths hand off to the fixed worker set, and the peak is 7 (Linux), 11 (Windows), 6 (macOS) at eight flows, unchanged at one flow. `landed` for the carriered legs that go through `lane_pool` (`relay_sink`, `relay_carried`, `relay_lanes_shared`): eight lanes at most, started on demand, a lane that moves bytes keeps its worker for a streak of eight steps and then rejoins the queue, an idle lane waits off a worker, and a socket-sourced lane waits on the descriptor its timed-out read named in the pool's readiness reaper, so the peer's byte wakes it (`a_socket_lane_waits_on_readiness`: 1 lane step in 250 ms with the seam, 15 with `park_ready` forced back to the timer; `an_idle_lane_waits_off_the_worker` gates the fd-less timer park), `a_narrow_cell_starts_only_the_lanes_it_queues` holds a two-lane cell to two workers, `a_saturated_cell_still_steps_the_lanes_behind_a_busy_one` fails when a lane that never parks strands the lanes behind it, and `every_lane_of_a_full_cell_has_a_worker` fails if the pool is narrower than the cell. `landed` for the VMess carriered pump: `pump_relay_carried` submits both directions to the same worker set and returns, `Wire::step` and the lazy server's `Answer` hand back an idle wait instead of parking, the carrier is armed at the relay quantum (`RelayPoll`) so its own read returns, and `a_live_vmess_session_costs_no_thread` holds eight live sessions without the task count moving while `eight_live_carried_sessions_share_one_pool` proves the pool still moves bytes when every session holds an idle leg. `landed` for both Shadowsocks pumps: `pump_relay_carried` submits the sealing leg and the opening leg to the same worker set and returns, `Downlink` steps the salt (or stream IV), the 2022 server's fixed header and its first chunk without waiting inside a read, `ChunkReader::try_fill` asks once and hands back a wait, and `a_live_session_costs_no_thread` holds eight live sessions per module without the task count moving. A carrier read that times out takes another turn rather than a parked quantum: the frame that grants the sibling writer's window arrives on that read, so parking it costs the writer a quantum and, armed at a stall, hung the `srv-ferrox` gRPC cell outright in run `40667696326`. `landed` for the rest of the pooled shape: `relay_stream` and `relay_shared` (`Half`) submit both directions to the same `lane_pool` and park an idle leg (`stream_lane_step` returns `Next::Idle`), and the carrier sink's upload leg parks the same way (`forward_finished`), while a carrier read never parks because the frame that grants the sibling's window arrives on it. The readiness seam is Unix-only (`Connection::read` notes the descriptor only under `cfg(unix)`); Windows keeps the timer, and every parked lane still takes one read-timeout step per `RELAY_RESPONSE_IDLE` for the `ended_and_idle` check, which is what remains of the clock edge. `landed` for the per-connection handshake on a TCP carrier: `accept_loop` submits the handshake and dispatch as a setup lane (`lane_pool::submit_setup`) on a two-worker setup pool separate from the eight relay lanes, with two more handshakes borrowing relay workers, so a parked handshake cannot take the whole relay set and the peak is bounded by those lanes rather than one thread per accepted connection; `carried_handshakes_share_the_lane_pool` holds an eight-flow cell to the setup lanes and their borrow ceiling, and `a_setup_lane_keeps_the_relay_step_off` proves the setup lane's waits stay on the handshake budget rather than the relay clock. Eight accepted setups may wait behind the four handshake slots; past that the handshake runs on its own thread, so a burst is slower, not refused, and a setup whose wait already spent the handshake budget closes instead of receiving a fresh one. A setup worker with no queued lane retires, so the setup threads are gone by the time a transfer is measured and do not sit beside the relay lanes. VLESS and Trojan UDP and mux sessions, VMess UDP associations, and Shadowsocks mux sessions are handed to their own thread after the request so they do not hold a setup worker. A silent peer occupies one of the four handshake slots while it waits, not a thread of its own. Trojan's TLS handshake runs on the relay lanes through `run_tls_handshake`, which arms the transport non-blocking so a step that finds no bytes parks its lane. `open` for the per-stream thread `serve_h2` spawns: it sits in a `read` until the peer's first bytes, so a cell's peak carries one thread per open xHTTP stream — the 18–19 against 12–15 in #696. No number here claims otherwise.
